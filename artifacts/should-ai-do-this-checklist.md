# Should AI do this? A before-you-start checklist
Run this before you hand a task to Claude. Work top to bottom. Any "no" or "not sure" is a stop sign: pause, do it yourself, or escalate to a human before you continue.

## The eight checks

### 1. Task suitability
* [ ] The task is something a language model is actually good at (drafting, summarizing, extracting, reshaping, explaining, coding).
* If no or unsure: the task may need a calculator, a database query, a human relationship, or a physical action instead. Stop and pick the right tool.

### 2. When not to use AI
* [ ] This is not a case where AI is the wrong call on principle: a legally binding decision made alone, medical or legal advice presented as authoritative, a judgment that must have a named human owner.
* If no or unsure: keep a human in the seat. Use AI as a draft or second opinion only, never the final word.

### 3. Authority
* [ ] You are allowed to do this thing at all (you have permission to send, publish, change, delete, or commit on behalf of whoever owns it).
* If no or unsure: get the green light from the owner first. AI does not grant you authority you did not already have.

### 4. Data sensitivity
* [ ] The information you are about to paste in is cleared for this tool (no secrets, customer PII, credentials, or confidential material that policy keeps out).
* If no or unsure: redact it, use an approved tool, or ask whoever owns the data. When in doubt, leave it out.

### 5. Stakes
* [ ] If the output is wrong, the damage is small and recoverable (not money lost, a relationship burned, or a reputation hit).
* If no or unsure: raise your effort, add a human reviewer, and verify every claim before anything ships.

### 6. Reversibility
* [ ] If this goes wrong, you can undo it (the send can be recalled, the file is backed up, the change is in version control).
* If no or unsure: treat it as one-way. Test on a copy first, add a confirmation step, and do not let AI pull the trigger unattended.

### 7. Required expertise
* [ ] You know enough about this subject to tell a good answer from a plausible wrong one. You can check the output yourself.
* If no or unsure: you cannot catch a confident mistake. Loop in someone who can verify, or learn enough first. Do not ship what you cannot check.

### 8. Required accounts and features
* [ ] You have the account, tier, tool, connector, or permission this task actually needs (the model, the integration, the paid feature, the access).
* If no or unsure: confirm access before you start, so you do not get halfway and stall. Request what is missing or route the task to someone who has it.

## The one-line rule
Clear it before you start. If every box is checked, go. If any box is not, stop or hand it to a human, then come back.
