# Level 1 cheat sheet: Foundations

The naming rule from each Level 1 lesson, in one page. One rule per lesson, plus the gist. Use it as a quick memory jog before you start a task.

## Should AI do this?
**Clear It Before You Start.** Scan eight checks before you hand over a task: suitability, when-not-to-use, authority, data sensitivity, stakes, reversibility, required expertise, required accounts and features. All clear, go. Any one "no" or "not sure", stop and fix the gap, hand it to a human, or escalate.

## Pick the right model
**Start Low, Climb on Evidence.** Ask three questions: one clear right answer (fast tier, low effort), ordinary judgment (everyday tier, default effort), or hard and high stakes (raise effort first, then climb a tier). Read the result and climb exactly one step if it falls short. Do not start at the top.

## Why you hit your limit
**Fresh Chat, Focused Handoff.** When Claude starts missing details or repeating itself, ask it to write a handoff of what you decided, what is open, key facts, and which files matter. Check the handoff, then open a new chat and paste it with only the files you still need. A fresh chat makes each turn cheaper and sharper, and does not reset your usage limit.

## Give Claude the right context
**Right Context, Not More Context.** Name the goal, the material it needs to see, the audience, and the limits. Include exactly the material needed and leave the rest out. Gut test: if you could not do the task with only what you pasted, Claude probably cannot either.

## Write a prompt like a spec
**Say the Finish Line.** A full prompt names five things: task, audience, details it must include, format, and how you will check it is good. You do not always need all five, but naming the finish line is what turns a vague ask into a spec.
