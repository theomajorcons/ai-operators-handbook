# Answer Check Card

Claude writes with the same calm confidence whether it is right or wrong. The
fluent tone is not evidence. Before you act on an answer, run this card on it.
You are not re-grading the whole thing; you are checking the claims the task
leans on.

**Goal:** Catch a wrong number, date, or fact before it leaves your hands and
goes out in your name.

---

## 1. Find the checkable claims

- [ ] **Numbers.** Totals, counts, percentages, amounts.
- [ ] **Dates and deadlines.** Anything with a calendar consequence.
- [ ] **Named facts.** "The only vendor that...", "nothing else calls this...",
  a quoted figure, a cited source.
- [ ] Skip the tone and phrasing. Those are either true or not is the test; an
  adjective is not a claim.

## 2. Pick the load-bearing ones

- [ ] Ask: **what would embarrass me if it were wrong?** That question finds the
  claim the decision rests on almost every time.
- [ ] Verify those first. A cosmetic error can wait; a wrong total that drives a
  budget ask cannot.

## 3. Verify independently

- [ ] **Go to the source.** Open the PDF, the CSV, the original doc.
- [ ] **Recompute.** Add the rows yourself; redo the math.
- [ ] **Cross-check.** Confirm the fact in a second place.
- [ ] Do **not** ask Claude "are you sure?" It can restate a wrong answer just as
  confidently. A real check goes outside the answer.

---

## Then decide

- **Use it** if the load-bearing claims held.
- **Fix it** if a claim failed but the rest is sound.
- **Drop it** and ask again with better context if the core is wrong, or if you
  cannot verify a claim the task depends on.

**One-line rule of thumb:** *Fluent is not correct. Check the few claims the
task leans on, independently, before you act.*
